## Notation decisions (binding on lanes 3 and 4 unless overturned)

N1. c is the correlation Cor(x, theta | M), as SPEC 1.2 defines it. The machine covariance with the outcome is g = c sqrt(s2_M / s2_T) = c sqrt(kappa / rel_H) times the human covariance, not c times it (SPEC 1.4 says "c times"; exact only when s2_M = s2_T). Lane 4's data-generating process must produce x | M with Cor(x, theta | M) = c and Var(x | M) = s2_M jointly, i.e. x | M = mu_M + c sqrt(s2_M / s2_T) theta + v with Var(v) = (1 - c^2) s2_M. If lane 4 instead codes x | M = mu_M + c theta + v, its c is my g and every closed form must be read with c -> g. Decide once, before Layer A pilot cells. Owner: lane 1 with lane 4 at day-2 handoff.

SPEC 1.1 defines rho = Cor(theta, w) and 1.3 says "rho_H, the validity coefficient in the human subpopulation"; the two readings differ by sqrt(rel_H). Lane 4's bias and RMSE metrics (SPEC 4.4) need to say which. Recommendation: rho_H as defined here for the headline, rho_T in the disattenuated appendix table. Owner: Abhay, one-line decision. ## Open questions

Q1 (SPEC-CORRECTION candidate). SPEC H1 says naive bias is "negative for validity coefficients in the small-mean-gap regime". Under ignorable authorship (A1) at c = 0, Prop. 1 shows the bias is negative for every mean gap; the mean gap only changes magnitude. The qualifier is needed only under non-ignorable authorship (section 4b). Suggested H1 wording: "negative for every mean gap under ignorable authorship; sign-flip possible only through the between-mean term of 4b". Owner: pass 1 (Astra) to confirm Prop. 1 independently, then SPEC edit.

Q2 (SPEC-CORRECTION candidate). SPEC 1.4 "net direction depends on the mean gap" is correct as a statement about direction relative to the pure-dilution benchmark 1 - pi (Prop. 2, crossover at kappa + (1 - pi) delta^2 = 1), not about the sign of the bias. Recommend the paper state both. Owner: writeup.

Q3. SPEC 1.2 "c near 1: contamination is nearly harmless". Props. 4 and 5 show c = 1 attenuates at small pi whenever c_2 = (1 + kappa) sqrt(rel_H) / (2 sqrt(kappa)) > 1, which holds under SPEC's own homogenization assumption (rel_H = 0.7, kappa = 0.2 gives c_2 = 1.12). Harmless requires c high and the machine output on the human scale (kappa near rel_H) and small mean gap. The paper's three-regime story needs this qualification or the amanuensis regime is overstated. Owner: lane 1 day 2 (write the qualified version), Abhay to decide whether the SPEC's regime language changes.

Q4. The outcome w is assumed clean (not machine-written). If w is itself an open-ended item scored the same way, it is a second mixture, the between-mean term appears in Cov(x, w) even under A1 (both means shift), and the sign-flip region of 4b opens up under ignorable authorship. This may be the realistic case for text-to-text validity (trait score against another text-scored construct). Not derived yet. Owner: lane 1 day 2 or 3, after Abhay says whether Layer B's validity target is a clean held-out score (SPEC 4.2 says "held-out trait or simulated outcome", which is clean).

Q5. Sign flips on the (pi, c) grid. Under A1 with c in [0, 1] every cell is sign-stable (Prop. 6). Flips need either negative c or the between-mean term under non-ignorable authorship (Prop. 7), and on the SPEC grid the latter needs delta d_theta > 3.33 sqrt(rel_H) at pi = 0.30. If the paper wants a sign-flip cell in the results, Layer A needs an arm with Delta_theta != 0 (LLM users differ on the trait), and the day-2 deletion-selection result supplies a mechanism that creates Delta_theta from an A1 population. Decision: add a non-ignorable-authorship arm to Layer A, or present sign stability as the finding. Owner: Abhay.

Q6. Magnitude of the pure deletion-selection bias (Prop. 11). At sp_bar = 0.85 and r_F = 0.3 the relative attenuation of rho_H is about 1.7 percent; at r_F = 0.5 about 5 percent; at sp_bar = 0.99 under 1 percent. On its own this is real but modest. The "kills detect-and-delete" argument (SPEC 1.5) will need the combined survivor-sample result of 6.4 (residual contamination plus manufactured between-mean term plus range restriction) and, probably, an empirical r_F from the essays testbed (fluency proxies against BFI scores; lane 3 can report this). Owner: lane 1 day 2 for the algebra; lane 3 for an r_F estimate.

Q7. Within-machine between-item correlation (Prop. 8) depends on rho_v, the shared machine residual across items, which the SPEC does not parameterize. Lane 3's counterpart generation determines it empirically (same session across items gives high rho_v; independent calls give rho_v near 0). Layer A needs rho_v as a grid parameter or a fixed value with justification. Owner: lane 3 to measure on generated counterparts; lane 4 to add to the DGP.

Q8. Location-shift selection (A1') keeps within-class trait variance equal. General selection P(M | theta) changes within-class variances too; the survivor-sample derivation (6.4) already needs unequal variances, so day 2 should derive the unequal-variance version once and have A1' as its special case. Owner: lane 1 day 2.

Q9. The deletion-selection result is stated for a Gaussian threshold detector. Lemma 1 is distribution-free for the mean shift; the variance result (E33) and the closed form (E34) are Gaussian. Pass 3 (Opus) should be briefed to attack whether a non-Gaussian fluency-trait relation could reverse the direction (it cannot change the mean-shift sign given monotone sp and positive regression dependence, but the variance reduction is not guaranteed in general). Owner: pass 3 brief.
