# Lane 2 pivot positioning — P1 / P4 vs. persona-conditioning literature

## Bottom line

- **P1 (coupling as estimand) collides directly with the persona-conditioning literature.** Its data source (Big5PersonalityEssays, GPT-4-turbo text conditioned on Big Five labels) and its measurement technique (trait-recoverability regression from text) are the same object and the same method PersonaLLM and Argyle-style work already study. This does not kill P1's novelty, but it removes the option of not citing that literature, and it narrows what the "coupling map" claim can still say.
- **P4 (no-generation reframe) avoids the collision by dropping generation entirely**, but at a larger cost: it demotes the coupling parameter from a modeled construct to background motivation, which is the piece of the day-1-cleared novelty position most exposed to erosion.
- Neither pivot changes the day-1 verdict on the two gate papers (Messing: no; SMR: no) — that verdict was about *claims*, not data provenance, and persona-conditioning literature was outside the day-1 gate's scope. It is now in scope because P1 and P4 both touch it, from opposite directions.

---

## What the persona-conditioning literature actually claims

Three programs are relevant, confirmed by this session's search (not previously checked against SPEC section 9's gate list, which named only the Messing paper, SMR, Psychometrika, and quantification methods):

- **PersonaLLM** (arXiv 2305.02547, ACL Findings NAACL 2024): prompts GPT-3.5 with 320 Big Five persona profiles, has each write an 800-word autobiographical story, and asks whether the resulting text carries linguistic markers consistent with the assigned trait. Finding: LLM output shows "emerging representative linguistic patterns" for personality, and human raters detect the intended trait at up to 80% accuracy — an accuracy that drops once raters are told the text is AI-authored.
- **Big Five persona-prompting / scaling work** (e.g. arXiv 2508.06149, "Scaling Personality Control in LLMs"; arXiv 2502.08265): treats trait-conditioned generation as a controllable-generation problem, asking how reliably a specified Big Five profile shows up in output as profile strength is scaled.
- **Argyle et al., "Out of One, Many"** (Political Analysis 2023, arXiv 2209.06899): conditions GPT-3 on real respondents' sociodemographic backstories and shows the model reproduces subgroup response *distributions* with "algorithmic fidelity" — a population-level silicon-sample claim, not an item-level authorship claim.

All three ask a **generation-fidelity question**: given a persona/trait specification, how well does the model's output express it? None of them asks the **measurement-correction question** this project owns: given that survey respondents (not researchers) silently substitute machine output at an unknown, uncontrolled degree of self-disclosure, how does that bias a validity coefficient between a latent trait and an external outcome, and can the bias be corrected without gold-standard authorship labels? PersonaLLM and the persona-prompting line evaluate the *generator*; Argyle's algorithmic fidelity is about *population-level* distributional match for research use, with the researcher in control of conditioning and aware of it. This project's mixture is about *individual-level*, respondent-initiated, unknown-to-the-researcher substitution — closer in spirit to SMR's problem statement than to Argyle's, but SMR (per the day-1 memo) never builds the correction.

## P1: coupling as estimand — collision assessment

P1 drops controlled generation and instead estimates per-item realized coupling c-hat from Big5PersonalityEssays via a trait-recovery model (audit3: ridge classifier on sentence embeddings, point-biserial r against the corpus's own Big Five labels, r = 0.11–0.24 across 4/5 traits, n.s. for openness). This is, mechanically, the same operation as PersonaLLM's linguistic-marker recovery: both ask "does text generated under a stated Big Five condition carry recoverable signal of that condition." The corpus itself (GPT-4-turbo, Big-Five-conditioned) is a direct product of the persona-conditioning tradition — using it makes this project's empirical coupling measurements a downstream reanalysis of persona-conditioned generation, not an independent construct.

Two consequences follow:

1. **The paper must explicitly cite and distinguish, not just cite.** A reviewer who knows PersonaLLM or Argyle will otherwise read "coupling parameter c, estimated from trait-recoverability" as a renamed algorithmic-fidelity score. The distinguishing move is available and should be made explicit in the writeup: PersonaLLM/Argyle ask whether conditioning *works*; this project treats the answer to that question as an *input measured with error* to an errors-in-variables correction of a validity coefficient the persona-conditioning literature never estimates. The contribution is the propagation step (c-hat with uncertainty → attenuation/reliability correction → corrected rho_H), not the recoverability measurement itself. That framing needs to appear on first mention of c-hat, not be left implicit.
2. **The empirical map shrinks to the range that exists in persona-conditioned corpora, which skews low.** Audit 3's r = 0.11–0.24 is far short of "c near 1" (SPEC 1.2's faithful-amanuensis case). Under P1, the paper can credibly speak to the low-coupling and part of the partial-coupling regime, not the high-coupling regime SPEC's grid was designed to reach. This weakens the boundary/identifiability story (SPEC 4.4, failure mode 1) — the project's "map of when contamination matters" becomes a map of a narrower c range than designed, and the c-high identifiability-collapse result (arguably the most defensible fallback finding per SPEC failure mode 1) has less empirical grounding to stand on, only theory.

Net effect on novelty: P1 is defensible but requires a paragraph the current draft plan doesn't yet have. It does not concede any claim to PersonaLLM/Argyle (their published results say nothing about validity-coefficient correction or prevalence), but it puts this project one citation-check away from looking derivative if that paragraph is skipped. Recommend treating "distinguish c-hat from algorithmic-fidelity/trait-expression scores, explicitly, in the methods section" as a hard requirement of P1, not a nice-to-have.

## P4: no-generation reframe — collision assessment

P4 needs no generation at all, so it never produces persona-conditioned text and never uses a persona-conditioning corpus as a load-bearing input. This **dodges** the direct methodological overlap P1 creates: there is no c-hat computed by the same technique PersonaLLM uses, because there is no controlled or measured coupling construct doing real work in the model — coupling is "demoted from experimental factor to measured moderator" (per the pivot prompt), meaning whatever coupling exists in the observational corpora is absorbed as unmodeled heterogeneity rather than an estimated covariate the paper reports on.

This is the safer position on the collision axis, but it does not fully exit the literature — the paper still needs the persona-conditioning line for a different job: **realism support**, not methodology. SPEC failure mode 4 ("nobody pastes survey items into ChatGPT") is countered today by citing SMR's homogenization evidence and the NORC/PMC identification studies; under P4, Argyle-style fidelity work becomes useful supporting evidence too — it establishes that LLMs *can* carry enough conditioned signal to matter when respondents do self-disclose, which backs the plausibility of the partial-coupling case the paper still describes qualitatively even without modeling it. So P4 trades "cite defensively to avoid being mistaken for this literature" (P1's burden) for "cite supportively to justify the story's plausibility without a model behind it" (a lighter, lower-risk citation job).

## Effect on the day-1 novelty position

The day-1 memo's verdict rested on three claims: (a) two-process mixture with coupling c, (b) label-free prevalence + validity-coefficient correction, (c) deletion-selection collider bias. Persona-conditioning literature was not in the day-1 gate and does not touch (b) or (c) under either pivot — no source in that literature does label-free prevalence estimation, errors-in-variables correction, or a collider-bias analysis of detection-based filtering. Both pivots leave (b) and (c) exactly as novel as the day-1 memo found them.

The pivots differ only on claim (a):

- **P1 keeps (a) alive but narrows it** to the coupling range recoverable from real persona-conditioned corpora (low, per audit 3), and makes its novelty conditional on explicitly out-flanking PersonaLLM/Argyle in the writeup rather than merely citing them. Risk is real but manageable; the fix is a paragraph, not a redesign.
- **P4 protects the novelty position by removing the exposure**, but at the cost of shrinking (a) from a modeled result to a qualitative moderator note — which is closer to where SMR's homogenization documentation already sits (SPEC's own day-1-cleared distinction from SMR was partly that SMR never formalizes coupling; under P4 this project also doesn't formalize it as an estimated quantity, just discusses it, which narrows — without eliminating — that distinction). The remaining edge over SMR under P4 is that (b) and (c) are still fully novel and still absent from SMR; (a) becomes supporting narrative rather than a third pillar.

**Recommendation for lane 1's memo**: P1 preserves more of the SPEC's original ambition on claim (a) and is defensible with one explicit methods-section paragraph distinguishing c-hat-as-measured-covariate from fidelity/expressiveness scores; take that cost rather than P4's larger claim-(a) demotion, unless the identifiability math in the design-pivot memo shows P1's narrow c-range makes claim (a) not worth defending on its own merits — that is lane 1's call, not a novelty-gate call.

## Q13 resolution — unconditioned generation arm in Big5PersonalityEssays / PersonaLLM

### Verdicts

- **Big5PersonalityEssays (arXiv 2407.17586): verified-absent.**
- **PersonaLLM (arXiv 2305.02547 / github.com/hjian42/PersonaLLM): verified-absent.**

Neither corpus has a same-generator, same-prompt arm with the persona/trait conditioning stripped out. Both are single-condition generation designs.

### Evidence

**Big5PersonalityEssays.** Paper PDF fetched directly (`arxiv.org/pdf/2407.17586`, 13 pages) and converted with `pdftotext -layout`. Section 5, "Dataset Generation" (extracted lines 230–251), is the only generation methodology described: "The model was instructed, using the prompt, to generate both text and labels... to generate a short passage in a role-play manner, behaving as a person with a personality described by the 5 labels... asked how a day in the life of that person would be." Every one of the 400 generated samples goes through this same role-play-on-5-labels prompt; there is no second condition. A full-text grep for `unconditioned|zero-shot|zero shot|baseline|control condition|without persona|no persona|neutral prompt` against the extracted text returned **zero matches**. The paper never mentions, runs, or even discusses a no-persona control as a possibility (not in Limitations or Future Work either — the extracted text has no such section beyond the one-paragraph conclusion at lines 347–362). The repo (`github.com/stnflri/Big5PersonalityEssays`, contents listed via `gh api repos/stnflri/Big5PersonalityEssays/contents/`) contains exactly one file, `Brief-State-of-Consciousness-Essays.xlsx` — no generation script, no prompt template, no second dataset. There is nothing left to check; the paper's prose is the entire specification of how the corpus was made, and it specifies one arm.

**PersonaLLM.** Repo file tree fetched via `gh api repos/hjian42/PersonaLLM/contents/` (tag `v2.0`): `run_bfi.py`, `run_creative_writing.py`, `prompts/{bfi_prompt.txt, bfi_scores.txt, writing_prompt.txt, llm-as-evaluator-prompts.zip}`. `run_creative_writing.py` (fetched raw, in full) has one code path, `generate_bfi_story()`, called from `main()` for every one of `itertools.product(*personality_types)` — the 32 combinations of the 5 bipolar Big Five trait pairs (`extroverted/introverted`, `agreeable/antagonistic`, etc., defined in `main()`). Inside `generate_bfi_story()`, the system prompt is built unconditionally as `"You are a character who is {}.".format(construct_big_five_words(persona_type))` — every single generated story carries a persona string; there is no branch, flag, or code path that calls the model with `prompts/writing_prompt.txt` alone (which itself is just the bare task, "Please write a story below in 800 words about your childhood," with `%BIGFIVE%`/`%ANSWER%` placeholders meant to be filled from the prior BFI-persona turn, not left empty). The loop only ever iterates over `personality_types`; there is no 33rd "no persona" condition and no `--baseline`/`--no-persona` CLI argument in `argparse` (the only arguments are `--prompt_file`, `--model`, `--temperature`). This is a direct code-level verdict, not an inference from a README.

### Why it matters

This empirically confirms, rather than changes, the reading already worked out analytically in `derivations/design-pivot-memo.md` section 3.2 (Prop. 16). That section already states the conclusion Q13 asks about: "the p = 0 arm is persona-conditioned text decorrelated from its recipient, not unconditioned text... An unconditioned generator would have a smaller Var(x | M), a lower kappa, and that difference is exactly the kappa-c covariation the design holds fixed by construction and the frozen design would have confounded with c." Confirming verified-absent on both corpora removes the possibility of resolving this by simply *finding* an unconditioned arm in existing data — it establishes that the assignment-coupling design's p=0 cell (random assignment of persona-conditioned text, decorrelating it from the recipient's label) is not a stand-in for a genuinely unconditioned generation arm but a deliberate, disclosed substitute, and that the kappa-c covariation the paper cannot observe is a real, uncloseable gap in the corpora available, not an oversight in the search. Practically: the paper must state this as a named limitation (Var(x|M) is fixed across the p-axis by construction, and no available corpus lets that vary with c), not imply the p=0 arm approximates SPEC 1.2's "faithful-amanuensis, zero-disclosure" case — it approximates "persona text whose label just doesn't happen to match the recipient," which is a different object with a different (larger, fixed) kappa than a true zero-coupling generator would have. This does not change P1's or P4's novelty position from the earlier pivot-positioning analysis above; it sharpens one sentence lane 1's memo already commits to, and closes off "look for a better corpus" as a way to avoid stating it.
